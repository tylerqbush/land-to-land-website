Most people find out the hard way.

They see a piece of land they want. Maybe it's cheap. Maybe it's exactly where they'd want to be someday. They call their bank, explain what they're looking at, and hear some version of: "We don't do loans on raw land."

Some banks will do it, with conditions. Forty percent down. Ten-year term. Rate two points above a house loan. And that's only if you have strong credit and the property is near a developed area. For most people buying rural vacant land, a traditional loan is simply not an option.

This isn't a niche problem. It's the #1 thing people run into when they try to buy land for the first time.

## Why banks say no

Banks make decisions based on how easily they can recover their money if something goes wrong.

A house is straightforward. It has an appraised value, it generates shelter (which people always need), and if the bank has to foreclose, there's a ready market of buyers. Raw land doesn't work the same way. There's no rental income. The value is harder to determine when there's nothing on the property. And if a bank has to foreclose on a vacant lot in a rural county, finding a buyer quickly is a real challenge.

So they either decline outright, or make the terms painful enough that most buyers walk away anyway.

## What owner financing is

Owner financing means the seller carries the note instead of a bank. You agree on a price and a down payment, sign a land contract, and make monthly payments directly to the seller until the balance is paid.

No bank in the middle. No underwriting committee reviewing your credit history. No 40% down payment. The seller sets the terms, and if those terms fit your budget, you have a deal.

This is how we sell land at Land to Land Holdings. You pick the property. We agree on a down payment and monthly payment that works for your situation. You sign a simple contract, make your payments, and the land is fully yours when it's paid off.

## What you actually own during the payment period

This is the question most buyers forget to ask.

When you buy on a land contract, you have equitable title. You can use the property, camp on it, and make improvements that don't require permits. The legal deed stays with us until the balance is paid in full, which means anything that requires a permit — building a structure, running utilities, that kind of work — isn't available to you yet.

That's worth being straight about. You're not in a position to break ground on a cabin during the payment period under a standard contract.

That said, we're not rigid about it. Buyers who have built a solid payment history have come to us wanting to do more with their land. In those cases we've worked out arrangements where we place a lien on the property, which protects us if something goes wrong and they default, and allows them to move forward with permitted work. It's handled case by case, based on the relationship.

For most buyers, the payment period is for camping, recreation, and light improvements. Full ownership and everything that comes with it arrives when the land is paid off.

## What happens if you miss a payment

We would rather work it out than foreclose. If something comes up, call us. We have always been willing to talk through a missed payment or a temporary hardship with a buyer who communicates.

The contract does have a default clause, same as any loan. But the goal is never to take the land back. The goal is for you to own it.

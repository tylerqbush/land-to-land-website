Some parcels don't tick the boxes.

The zoning says one thing, the lot size says another, and by the time you do the math, you're looking at a piece of land that's too small to camp on legally, can't fit an RV under county rules, and won't qualify for anything the permitting office will touch.

First reaction: pass.

Worth a second look before you do.

---

**What you actually own**

A deed with your name on it. A parcel number in the county records. A piece of the earth that belongs to you and nobody else.

Land appreciates. Not every year, not in every market — but over time, rural land has gone up. A small parcel bought cheaply in a growing area doesn't appreciate less than a large one. Sometimes it appreciates more in percentage terms, because the entry price was so low that any movement matters.

The county doesn't put a minimum on whose name goes in the deed.

---

**What you can do with it**

**Hold it.** The simplest play, and often the right one. You own it, it sits, the market does what markets do. No maintenance calls. No tenants. No one calling you on a Thursday night about something that is definitely the water heater.

**Lease it.** Adjacent landowners often want land that borders theirs more than you'd expect. Grazing leases, access arrangements, storage use — a neighbor whose fence line runs up against yours has a reason to pay you for what you can't use yourself. A few hundred dollars a year isn't life-changing. It's more than nothing, which is what the land generates if you don't ask.

**Aggregate it.** A lot of buyers start small on purpose. They buy the affordable parcel first, then watch what comes available next door. Two non-conforming lots combined can clear the minimum. One small parcel becomes the anchor for a larger position. The first purchase is the foothold.

**Visit it.** This one gets dismissed, and shouldn't be. You can go out there. Walk it. Stand on it and know what it looks like in different light, different seasons. The experience of owning a piece of ground doesn't require a campfire or a platform tent. It just requires showing up.

---

**Who this is for**

Not everyone, and we'll say that directly.

If you're buying land because you want somewhere to be this summer — a campsite, a weekend destination, a place to park an RV and disappear — a small non-conforming parcel is the wrong choice for you. We'd rather tell you that than sell you something that doesn't fit what you're actually after.

But if you're buying as an investment, as a first step toward something larger, or because you want your name on a deed in a place that matters to you — a small parcel does exactly that. The deed is real. The appreciation is real. The options it opens later are real.

The size is just the size.

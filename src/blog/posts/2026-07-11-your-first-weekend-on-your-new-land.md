The GPS takes you off the paved road about three miles from the coordinates.

That's how you know you're close.

There's a specific feeling when the phone says "your destination is on the right" and what's on your right is just land. No address. No mailbox. No house visible from the road. You slow down, check the pin again, and pull over somewhere that seems right.

Then you get out of the car.

The first thing you notice is the quiet. Not silence exactly, just the absence of the kind of noise you've stopped noticing. No traffic. No one else's music bleeding through a wall. Whatever was filling your head on the drive out starts to drain away, and what's left is the sound of the place.

You walk the perimeter. You'll probably do it twice. Not because you need to, but because you can, and because it starts to feel real when you do it.

---

**What most people do the first weekend**

Set up camp somewhere that feels right. (The first time, you'll probably move it once.)

Start a fire before dark, because starting a fire before dark is easier than starting one after dark. That sounds obvious until you're finding out the hard way.

Cook something simple over it. Not because you have to, but because food cooked over a fire on your own land tastes different than it does anywhere else. That's not a metaphor. It actually tastes different.

Walk around at night with a flashlight. Sit somewhere and look up.

Sleep there if you can. Wake up to whatever the land sounds like at first light, which is different from what it sounds like at noon, which is different from dusk. By the end of the weekend you'll know all three.

None of this costs anything extra. All of it belongs to you.

---

**The part people don't expect**

Somewhere in the middle of the first weekend, usually during something low-stakes like collecting firewood or just standing somewhere looking at nothing, something shifts.

You stop thinking of it as "the land" and start thinking of it as yours.

It happens faster than people expect. It's a small shift and a real one.
